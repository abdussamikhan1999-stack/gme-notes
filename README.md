# GME (/GME/) Notes

Notes distilled from `/GME/` (a GameStop-focused thread, 4chan-style) — the
2021 short squeeze, DRS (Direct Registration), and the community's ongoing
theories about the situation. Informational only, not financial advice —
and this thread in particular mixes documented fact with unverified
speculation more than most in this series, so I've split them explicitly.

See [LINKS.md](LINKS.md) for the full raw link list.

## What's actually documented (2021 events)

- GameStop was one of the most heavily shorted stocks on the market in
  early 2021; a coordinated wave of retail buying (largely via Robinhood
  and organized on Reddit's r/wallstreetbets) drove the price up over
  1,000% in January 2021, threatening the short sellers with major losses.
- **GameStop genuinely sat on the SEC's "threshold list"** (a real,
  official list of stocks with persistent settlement failures) for 39
  consecutive trading days, with hundreds of millions of shares in "fails
  to deliver" (FTDs — a broker sold a share it didn't actually deliver on
  time). This part is documented regulatory fact, not community theory.
  The **SEC's own staff report** on the episode
  (`sec.gov/files/staff-report-equity-options-market-struction-conditions-early-2021.pdf`)
  is the primary source to read here rather than any secondary summary.
- On January 28, 2021, Robinhood restricted *buying* GME (and several other
  meme stocks) while still allowing selling — a real, heavily scrutinized
  event that triggered congressional hearings and multiple lawsuits.
  Robinhood's stated reason was a sudden spike in clearinghouse collateral
  requirements; critics alleged conflict-of-interest coordination with
  Citadel Securities (which processes Robinhood's order flow). Both the
  official explanation and the suspicion of coordination are documented
  positions — which one you find more credible is a judgment call, not
  something settled by the public record.
- **Direct Registration (DRS) via Computershare** is a real, legitimate
  mechanism: instead of your broker holding shares in "street name" (the
  broker is the registered owner, you have a claim against the broker),
  DRS registers *you* directly with the transfer agent as the shareholder
  of record. The SEC's own investor-education material describes this as
  a legitimate option distinct from broker custody — it's a genuine choice
  with real tradeoffs (no easy day-trading of DRS'd shares, but no broker
  counterparty risk either), not itself a conspiracy theory.
- Computershare account numbers for GameStop specifically went from ~40,000
  in August 2021 to 715,000+ by October 2021 — a real, verifiable jump in
  direct registration, whatever you conclude it means.

## Where documented fact ends and community theory begins

The thread's framing goes well beyond the above into claims that are
**community theory, not established fact**:

- **"What happens if Citadel becomes insolvent... DTCC with $60T... FDIC as
  the insurance"** — this specific chain of claims (an imminent Citadel
  insolvency event, a "MOASS"-style resolution, DTCC/FDIC backstopping it)
  is speculative community narrative, not a documented or imminent
  regulatory event. Treat it as such.
- **"Naked short selling and systemic risk"** as an ongoing, deliberately
  covered-up practice specifically targeting GameStop years after 2021 is
  an unresolved, contested claim — naked short selling is a real
  regulatory concept the SEC has written about and enforced against in
  specific cases, but the leap to "it's still happening to GME right now,
  covertly, at scale" is the community's inference, not a documented
  finding.
- **"Cellar boxing"** and similar mechanism-specific theories (in the
  linked Reddit DD posts) are community-originated financial theories, not
  peer-reviewed or regulator-confirmed mechanisms. Reddit itself carries an
  explicit disclaimer in this thread — *"reddit DDs don't take them for
  fact use your brain"* — which is honestly the most important piece of
  guidance in the whole OP.

## Stag hunt (the game-theory reference)

The thread links Wikipedia's **stag hunt** article as a framing device: a
game-theory scenario where a group is collectively better off cooperating
toward an ambitious shared goal (hunting a stag together) than each acting
individually for a smaller guaranteed payoff (hunting a hare alone) — it
has two stable equilibria (all-cooperate or all-defect), unlike the
prisoner's dilemma's single equilibrium. The community uses this as a
metaphor for coordinated holding/buying behavior; it's a legitimate,
well-established game theory concept being used as a rhetorical frame here,
not itself a claim about GameStop.

## Bottom line

This thread is a mix of real, checkable regulatory history (the 2021
squeeze, the threshold list, DRS mechanics) and an active, unresolved
community theory about ongoing manipulation and an eventual reckoning. The
thread's own "reddit DDs aren't fact" disclaimer is worth applying to the
whole OP, including the parts that don't come from Reddit.
